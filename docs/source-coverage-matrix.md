## 2. Textbook 1: Baran & Galka (2017) Detailed Structure

*Customer Relationship Management: The Foundation of Contemporary Marketing Strategy* (2nd Edition, Routledge, 489 pages).

### Part I: CRM Theory and Development
- **Chapter 1: Introduction to Customer Relationship Management (PDF pp. 8–47 / Book pp. 1–38)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Definitions of CRM (strategic, operational, analytical, collaborative), transactional vs. relationship marketing, customer lifetime value foundations, customer equity, value creation processes.
  - *Readability:* Verified clean text stream, complete headings and sub-headings.
- **Chapter 2: The History and Development of CRM (PDF pp. 48–80 / Book pp. 39–70)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Evolution from direct marketing, database marketing, sales force automation (SFA) to modern enterprise CRM; impact of Internet and e-CRM.
- **Chapter 3: Relationship Marketing and CRM (PDF pp. 81–112 / Book pp. 71–101)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Relationship commitment, trust, reciprocity, social bonding, structural bonding, switching costs, relational norms.
- **Chapter 4: Organization and CRM (PDF pp. 113–139 / Book pp. 102–127)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Customer-centric organizational design, cross-functional alignment, culture transformation, change management, employee empowerment.

### Part II: Data Management and Technology
- **Chapter 5: CRM and Data Management (PDF pp. 140–179 / Book pp. 128–166)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Customer data types (demographic, behavioral, psychographic, transactional), data hygiene, data warehousing, ETL processes, single customer view (SCV).
- **Chapter 6: Technology and Data Platforms (PDF pp. 180–207 / Book pp. 167–193)**
  - *Core Concepts:* CRM IT architecture, Cloud/SaaS vs. On-Premise, analytical CRM tools, business intelligence, data mining algorithms, API integrations.
- **Chapter 7: Database and Customer Data Development (PDF pp. 208–238 / Book pp. 194–223)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Relational database design for CRM, customer identification, data quality management, privacy by design, consent management.

### Part III: Marketing Strategy
- **Chapter 8: Business-to-Business CRM (PDF pp. 239–269 / Book pp. 224–253)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Key Account Management (KAM), B2B buying center dynamics, complex sales cycles, relational contracting, joint value creation.
- **Chapter 9: Understanding the Customer–Company Profit Chain: Satisfaction, Loyalty, Retention, and Profits (PDF pp. 270–295 / Book pp. 254–278)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Service-profit chain, customer satisfaction metrics (CSAT, NPS), behavioral vs. attitudinal loyalty, customer retention economics, churn prediction.
- **Chapter 10: The CRM Strategy Cycle: Acquisition, Retention, and Win-Back (PDF pp. 296–316 / Book pp. 279–298)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Customer lifecycle management, acquisition strategies, onboarding, cross-selling/upselling, defection detection, win-back strategies and ROI.
- **Chapter 11: Privacy and Ethics Considerations (PDF pp. 317–350 / Book pp. 299–332)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Consumer privacy rights, GDPR/regulatory frameworks, ethical data harvesting, surveillance capitalism concerns, trust erosion risks.

### Part IV: CRM Evaluation
- **Chapter 12: CRM Program Measurement and Tools (PDF pp. 352–397 / Book pp. 333–377)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) calculation models, Customer Acquisition Cost (CAC), Return on Customer Investment (ROCI), dashboard KPI design.

### Part V: CRM New Horizons
- **Chapter 13: Social Networking and CRM (PDF pp. 398–430 / Book pp. 378–409)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Social CRM (SCRM), conversational commerce, listening and sentiment analysis, community co-creation, influencer relationship management.
- **Chapter 14: CRM Trends, Challenges, and Opportunities (PDF pp. 431–451 / Book pp. 410–430)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Artificial intelligence and machine learning in predictive CRM, omnichannel customer experience orchestration, ethical governance, emerging frontiers.

## 3. Textbook 2: Kostojohn, Johnson & Paulen (2011) Detailed Structure

*CRM Fundamentals* (Apress, 244 pages).

- **Chapter 1: Introduction (PDF pp. 14–23 / Book pp. 1–10)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Tactical vs. strategic definitions, sources of business value (productivity, learning organization, better customer experience, decision support), data-driven culture prerequisites, executive sponsorship.
- **Chapter 2: Components of CRM Success (PDF pp. 24–60 / Book pp. 11–46)**
  - *Core Concepts:* The "People, Process, Technology" triad; governance structure (Executive Sponsor, Steering Committee, Implementation Team, Departmental Champions, CRM Admin Team); operational vs. maintenance vs. enhancement processes; environment management; failure pitfalls.
- **Chapter 3: Building a CRM Roadmap (PDF pp. 61–76 / Book pp. 47–62)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Phased implementation strategy, value vs. complexity prioritization matrix, scope bounding, roadmap midstream adaptation.
- **Chapter 4: Evaluating Software and Consultants (PDF pp. 77–104 / Book pp. 63–90)**
  - *Core Concepts:* CRM vendor landscape (Enterprise, Mid-Market, SMB), Cloud vs. On-Premises trade-offs, RFP processes, consultant selection criteria and contract traps.
- **Chapter 5: Planning the Initial CRM Implementation (PDF pp. 105–141 / Book pp. 91–126)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Project management methodologies (Waterfall vs. Agile in CRM), budgeting, resource allocation, risk registers, change management frameworks.
- **Chapter 6: Executing the Initial CRM Implementation (PDF pp. 142–207 / Book pp. 127–192)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Detailed design stage, custom development governance, system integration patterns, reporting & BI architecture, data migration strategy (cleansing, mapping, validation), multi-tier testing (unit, system, integration, UAT, performance), cutover & go-live playbooks.
- **Chapter 7: Maintaining and Evolving CRM (PDF pp. 208–235 / Book pp. 193–220)**
  - *Core Concepts:* Post-launch steering committee cadence, user adoption tracking, support tiering, continuous release management, ecosystem monitoring.
