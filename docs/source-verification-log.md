## 3. In-Depth Source Investigation: The "80/20 Rule" vs. "Four Pillars"

### 3.1. Background of the Investigation
Prior industry lore frequently asserts that "CRM is 80% People & Process and only 20% Technology." The audit specifically examined whether Kostojohn et al. (2011) explicitly state an exact numerical 80/20 ratio.

### 3.2. PyPDF Textual Verification of Kostojohn et al. (2011)
- **Target Section:** Chapter 2 (*The Four Pillars of CRM Success*, printed pp. 11–28, PDF pp. 25–42).
- **Extracted Text Findings:**
  Kostojohn, Paul, and Johnson state that the overwhelming majority of failed CRM implementations failed because executive leadership treated CRM as a software installation rather than an organizational transformation. They define **Four Equal Pillars**:
  1. **People:** User buy-in, training, role definitions, and cultural readiness.
  2. **Process:** Documented, standardized customer-facing workflows prior to automation.
  3. **Technology:** The enabling software tool, database, and infrastructure.
  4. **Expectations:** Realistic executive and end-user expectations regarding ROI timelines and deliverables.

### 3.3. Academic Clarification in Study Materials
The hub accurately contextualizes this finding:
> *Source Fact:* Kostojohn et al. formulate the **Four Pillars of CRM Success** (*People, Process, Technology, Expectations*) to explain why technology alone fails.  
> *Consulting Synthesis / Mnemonic:* While industry practitioners colloquially summarize this principle as "80% People & Process, 20% Technology," Kostojohn's actual framework treats all four pillars as interdependent, non-substitutable foundations of project success.

## 5. Distinction Between Source Facts and Pedagogical Extensions

To ensure full transparency:

1. **Source Facts (Tier 1 - Highest Authority):**
   - Direct definitions, formulas, empirical business metrics, and textbook quotes.
   - Identified by explicit book and page citations (e.g., `Baran & Galka (2017), p. 225`).
2. **Pedagogical Synthesis (Tier 2):**
   - Structuring chapter study notes into Learning Objectives, Core Concepts, Key Takeaways, and Review Questions.
   - Preserves 100% fidelity to the author's meaning while standardizing pedagogical delivery for effective self-study.
3. **Supplementary Extensions (Tier 3):**
   - Business frameworks applied to case studies (e.g., calculating CLV scenarios, SWOT matrices, Step-by-step problem-solving rubrics).
   - Designed to challenge learners in exam-style critical thinking without altering underlying empirical facts.
